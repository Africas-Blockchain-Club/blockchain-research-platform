## Phase 1.5: Exchange Pool Maths

**What a liquidity pool is.** Many decentralised exchanges (DEXs: exchanges run by smart contracts rather than a company) do not match buyers with sellers. Instead they use a **liquidity pool**: a smart contract holding two tokens, for example TIA and the USDC stablecoin. Anyone can trade against the pool.

**The constant product formula.** The pool keeps one rule: the amount of the first token (x) multiplied by the amount of the second token (y) must stay the same number (k). Written plainly: x * y = k. The price of TIA in the pool is y / x.

**Why it matters.** When someone buys TIA, they take TIA out (x falls) and put USDC in (y rises). The pool must keep x * y equal to k, so each extra TIA becomes more expensive. This is called **slippage** (the difference between the price you expect and the average price you actually get).

**The 10% example, worked through in general terms.** Real pool balances are not available, because DexScreener returned a "404" error (the page or record was not found). This means the formula cannot be worked through with TIA's actual numbers. The 10% case can still be shown in general terms, because the result does not depend on the pool's size:

1. Starting point: x TIA and y USDC. Price = y / x.
2. A buyer takes out 10% of the TIA. TIA left = 0.9 * x.
3. The USDC side must become k / (0.9 * x) = y / 0.9, which is about 1.111 * y.
4. New price = (y / 0.9) / (0.9 * x) = y / (0.81 * x), which is about 1.235 * (y / x).

So removing 10% of the TIA side raises the pool price by about 23.5%. The buyer paid an average of about 11.1% above the starting price, because they received 0.1 * x TIA for about 0.111 * y USDC.

**Why the pool maths may not describe TIA's main market.** The data marks pool liquidity as unknown (liquidity_reported is null), not as zero. It also shows that most measured trading depth sits on centralised exchanges. These exchanges use an **order book** (a list of buyers and sellers posting orders at prices they choose). An order book does not follow x * y = k; the price moves only as far as the posted orders run out. Missing pool data therefore does not mean TIA cannot be traded. It means this particular source did not find or return a pool.

### Not covered by these checks

No free, reliable source answers these, so the platform does not measure them. Check them yourself:

- **Token unlocks in the next 30 days.** Dates on which locked tokens become free to trade. Large unlocks often add selling pressure around the unlock date. Where to look: The project's own vesting documents, or a token-unlock tracker such as Tokenomist.
- **Supply held by a few wallets.** When a few wallets hold most of the supply, one decision by one holder can move the price. Where to look: A block explorer's top-holders list. Remember that exchange and contract wallets hold coins for many people.
